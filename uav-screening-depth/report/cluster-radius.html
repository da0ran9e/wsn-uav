T_hop measures in single-digit seconds — R* lands outside 94–400 m

The probe had three possible outcomes. The measurement picks the second: T_hop = 1.51–2.80 s at k = 8 over R = 94–300 m, against the ≥ 6.91 s that would be needed to bring R* inside 400 m. The design rule becomes make clusters as large as the kinematics allow, not “tune R to an interior optimum”.

  • Contention was the wrong suspect. The hypothesis behind the "tens of seconds" estimate was that hundreds of nodes sharing a 5 packet/s channel would serialise. They do not: MAC drop rates stay below a few per cent and rise only mildly with density, while completion time barely moves. What sets T_hop is the advertise-then-push round trip, not channel saturation.
  • T_hop falls with R, exponent -0.63 [-0.84, -0.44], R² = 0.97. The closed form assumes T_hop constant in R, so it is an approximation, not a law.
  • A pre-registered prediction failed, and it was mine. I predicted T_hop would scale as k/λ with exponent near 1. Measured: +0.28 [+0.25, +0.32], R² = 0.99 at R = 150 m — quadrupling k multiplies T_hop by only about 1.5×. Prediction 3.2 is REFUTED. See below for what the data says instead.
  • Spatial reuse absorbs the R² traffic growth: T_spread exponent in n_c is – at R = 150 m, while MAC drops rise over the same range.
  • The kinematic bound R ≥ 4ρ/3 = 94.1 m binds hard below itself: realised tours are –× the BHH estimate above it but –× below, a – % penalty. It is not the active constraint, because T_total falls in R throughout.
  • B5 not measured yet — the seed-only arm did not run.

Predictions were registered in docs/PREREGISTRATION-cluster-radius.md and committed before these sweeps were read. Scorecard at the end.

The k/λ floor does not bind — a refuted prediction

I pre-registered the mechanism as the per-node send pacing: moving k fragments across one ring should cost at least k/λ = k × 200 ms, so T_hop should scale with k at exponent ≈ 1. The measurement refutes that: the exponent is +0.28 [+0.25, +0.32], R² = 0.99 at R = 150 m and +0.19 [+0.19, +0.19], R² = 1.00 at R = 250 m.

The data also says why. At k = 16 and R ≥ 150 m, T_hop is at most 2.25 s — below the 3.2 s a single node would need to emit 16 fragments. A ring is therefore served by several nodes pushing different fragments concurrently, so per-node pacing never becomes the per-ring cost once n_c ≫ k. Consistent with that, packets per node rise roughly linearly in k (see the table) while completion time does not.

This strengthens the headline conclusion rather than weakening it, and it closes the one escape hatch the pre-registration left open. I had noted that k = 16 with a slow advertisement regime might lift T_hop to the 6.91 s needed for an interior R*. It does not: T_hop is far less sensitive to k than the floor argument assumed.

The red line is what I predicted and is wrong. Measured T_hop is far flatter, and at k = 16 it sits BELOW the per-node floor -- which is only possible if several nodes serve one ring concurrently.

Curvature. Every realised tour was sampled at 2 m arc-length steps and checked numerically: the worst |κ|·ρ over all 0 tours is None. Dubins arcs saturate the constraint exactly — that is what a value of 1 means — and nothing exceeds it, so no tour is kinematically infeasible.

What this probe does not establish

  • T_hop is protocol-dependent. It is reported as a function of R, n_c, k and the advertisement interval. A materially different dissemination protocol would move it. What is protocol-independent is the k/λ floor, and that floor alone already exceeds the contention term.
  • One cell, not a network. No UAV, no trajectory, no air-to-ground link, no inter-cluster traffic. That was the point of the shortcut, but cross-cell interference is absent.
  • The sweep is reduced to 45 configurations, not the full 7×3×3×2 factorial. N = 120 seeds per configuration was held fixed — the factorial was cut, never N.
  • T1 uses a hex-tiling stand-in for Phase 0, not PECEE: cluster heads are the node nearest each occupied cell centre. A real CH election would move the stops, though not the 1/R scaling.
  • The composed CI treats the two terms as independent, because they come from separate seed sets.
  • No claim about R > 400 m beyond “the curve is still falling”. The flight grid stops there.
